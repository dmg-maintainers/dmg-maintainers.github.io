abstract: 'Summary: The conditional gradient method is attractive when linear minimization
  over the feasible region is substantially cheaper than projection. Its classical
  convergence theory, however, is formulated for compact feasible sets, whereas many
  natural convex feasible regions are closed and unbounded. This paper studies a simple
  compact-restriction principle for applying conditional gradient steps to unbounded
  feasible regions. The first scheme uses one compact convex set containing the initial
  objective sublevel set. The second scheme updates the restriction by intersecting
  compact convex sets generated along the iterations. In both cases the linear minimization
  oracle is solved only over compact subsets, but the resulting objective values converge
  to the global optimum of the original problem, provided the compact restrictions
  contain the corresponding objective sublevel sets. For smooth convex objectives
  we obtain the standard superlinear convergence rate of the objective. We also record
  constructive restrictions based on strong convexity, exact sublevel sets, and epigraph
  caps, and include a nonsmooth conditional subgradient extension with a sublinear
  convergence rate under a curvature assumption. Numerical experiments illustrate
  the behaviour of the fixed and dynamic restrictions on unbounded feasible regions.'
